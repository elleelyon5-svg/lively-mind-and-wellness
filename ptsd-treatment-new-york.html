PTSD • NEW YORK

PTSD Support and Care

After a traumatic experience, some people continue to experience distressing memories, avoidance, changes in mood, or feeling constantly on guard. Support can begin with a careful assessment.

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) may involve intrusive memories or nightmares, avoidance of reminders, negative changes in thoughts or mood, and heightened alertness after exposure to trauma. Trauma responses are individual, and symptoms can overlap with other conditions. A qualified clinician can assess symptoms and discuss appropriate options.

ASSESSMENT & CARE

Trauma-informed assessment

An assessment may explore symptoms, their effect on sleep and relationships, current coping strategies, safety, and treatment preferences. Evidence-based trauma-focused psychotherapies may be appropriate for some people; medication evaluation or management may also be considered based on individual needs.

NEXT STEPS

Respectful, collaborative care

You should not have to share more detail about a traumatic event than you feel ready to discuss during an initial conversation. Treatment is individualized, and the pace should reflect clinical needs and your preferences. For immediate danger or crisis, call 911 or call/text 988 in the United States.
