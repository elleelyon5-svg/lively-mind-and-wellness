ADJUSTMENT DISORDER • NEW YORK

Adjustment Disorder Support

Major life changes or stressful events can affect mood, behavior, and everyday functioning. Support can help you understand your response and identify next steps.

Adjustment disorder involves emotional or behavioral symptoms in response to an identifiable stressor. Reactions may include anxiety, sadness, worry, difficulty concentrating, or changes in functioning. A clinician considers the timing, impact, context, and other possible explanations before making a diagnosis.

ASSESSMENT & CARE

Make sense of a difficult transition

An assessment may explore the stressor, when symptoms began, how daily life has changed, your current supports, and whether another condition better explains the symptoms. Depending on your needs, care may include psychotherapy, coping strategies, and medication evaluation when clinically appropriate.

NEXT STEPS

Build a support plan

Support can focus on problem-solving, stress management, restoring routines, and strengthening coping resources. If symptoms become severe, persist, or interfere substantially with daily functioning, seek professional assessment. For a mental health crisis in the United States, call or text 988.
